# Employee Handbook

## Document Information

- **Document title:** Employee Handbook
- **Document version:** 1.0
- **Effective date:** January 1, 2026
- **Organization:** Example Technologies
- **Document purpose:** This handbook explains the organization's workplace expectations, employee responsibilities, benefits, policies, and common procedures.

---

## 1. Welcome to Example Technologies

Example Technologies is a technology company that develops software products and provides digital services. The organization values professionalism, collaboration, integrity, continuous learning, customer focus, and respect.

This handbook is intended to help employees understand how the organization operates and where to find guidance about common workplace situations.

This handbook provides general guidance. It does not replace an employment contract, applicable law, collective agreements, or written policies issued by Human Resources.

---

## 2. Our Core Values

### 2.1 Integrity

Employees are expected to act honestly, protect company information, disclose conflicts of interest, and communicate accurately.

### 2.2 Respect

Employees must treat colleagues, customers, suppliers, and visitors with dignity. Harassment, discrimination, bullying, intimidation, and retaliation are not acceptable.

### 2.3 Accountability

Employees are responsible for the quality and timeliness of their work. When a problem occurs, employees should communicate it early and participate in finding a solution.

### 2.4 Collaboration

Employees should share knowledge, support colleagues, document important decisions, and work constructively across departments.

### 2.5 Continuous Improvement

Employees are encouraged to learn new skills, request feedback, improve processes, and identify opportunities to reduce errors and improve customer outcomes.

---

## 3. Employment Responsibilities

Employees are expected to:

- Perform the duties described in their role.
- Follow lawful and reasonable instructions.
- Meet agreed deadlines and quality standards.
- Attend required meetings and training.
- Keep their manager informed about progress, risks, and blockers.
- Protect company property and information.
- Follow security, privacy, and workplace-safety procedures.
- Maintain accurate records where required.
- Treat customers and colleagues professionally.

Managers are expected to:

- Set clear expectations.
- Provide useful feedback.
- Support employee development.
- Apply policies consistently.
- Escalate serious concerns to Human Resources.
- Protect employees from retaliation when they raise concerns in good faith.

---

## 4. Working Hours and Attendance

Employees should follow the working schedule specified in their employment agreement or department policy.

Employees are expected to:

- Start work on time.
- Be available during agreed working hours.
- Notify their manager as soon as possible if they will be late or absent.
- Record working time accurately when time tracking is required.
- Attend scheduled meetings or notify the organizer when they cannot attend.

Repeated lateness, unexplained absence, or inaccurate time reporting may lead to corrective action.

For remote employees, availability should be communicated through the approved communication channels. Employees should keep their calendars and status information reasonably accurate.

---

## 5. Remote and Hybrid Work

Remote and hybrid work may be available depending on the employee's role, performance, team requirements, and business needs.

Employees working remotely must:

- Maintain a suitable and secure working environment.
- Protect company equipment from unauthorized access.
- Use approved communication and collaboration tools.
- Attend required meetings.
- Maintain reasonable availability during agreed working hours.
- Avoid discussing confidential company matters in public places.
- Report connectivity, equipment, or security problems promptly.

Remote work is not permission to perform outside employment during scheduled company working hours unless explicitly authorized.

---

## 6. Leave and Time Off

Employees should request planned leave through the approved process and obtain the required approval before making commitments that depend on the leave.

Common categories of leave may include:

- Annual vacation.
- Public holidays.
- Sick leave.
- Family or personal leave.
- Parental leave.
- Bereavement leave.
- Other legally required or company-approved leave.

The amount of leave, eligibility requirements, notice periods, and supporting documents depend on applicable law, the employment agreement, and company policy.

When an employee is unexpectedly unable to work, they should notify their manager and Human Resources as soon as reasonably possible.

---

## 7. Compensation and Payroll

Employees are paid according to the terms of their employment agreement.

Employees should review their payslips and report suspected errors to Human Resources or Payroll promptly.

Payroll questions may include:

- Salary calculation.
- Allowances.
- Overtime where applicable.
- Approved deductions.
- Benefits deductions.
- Bank-account changes.
- Tax or statutory contributions.

Employees must not request salary payments to an account that they are not authorized to use. Changes to payment information must follow the organization's verification procedure.

---

## 8. Performance and Professional Development

Performance is evaluated using role expectations, agreed objectives, quality of work, collaboration, reliability, customer impact, and professional conduct.

The performance process may include:

1. Goal setting.
2. Regular one-to-one meetings.
3. Feedback from managers and colleagues.
4. Mid-period progress reviews.
5. Formal performance reviews.
6. Development planning.

Employees are encouraged to discuss:

- Career goals.
- Training needs.
- Technical skills.
- Communication skills.
- Leadership opportunities.
- Workload concerns.
- Areas where they need support.

Performance concerns should be addressed early, clearly, and fairly.

---

## 9. Code of Conduct

Employees must act professionally and avoid behavior that could damage the organization or its relationships.

Prohibited or unacceptable conduct may include:

- Theft or fraud.
- Falsification of records.
- Bribery or corruption.
- Harassment or discrimination.
- Threats or violence.
- Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information.
- Deliberate misuse of company systems.
- Conflicts of interest that are not disclosed.
- Working while impaired in a way that creates a safety or performance risk.
- Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

Employees who are unsure whether an action is appropriate should consult their manager, Human Resources, Legal, or the designated ethics contact.

---

## 10. Anti-Harassment and Equal Opportunity

Example Technologies aims to provide a workplace free from discrimination, harassment, and retaliation.

Employment decisions should be based on legitimate business and role-related criteria. The organization does not tolerate unfair treatment based on characteristics protected by applicable law.

Harassment can include:

- Offensive comments or jokes.
- Unwanted sexual attention.
- Repeated humiliating behavior.
- Threatening or intimidating conduct.
- Unwelcome messages or images.
- Exclusion intended to punish or degrade someone.
- Abuse of authority.

Employees may report concerns to:

- Their manager.
- Human Resources.
- A designated ethics or compliance contact.
- Another reporting channel identified by company policy.

Reports should be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Retaliation for making a good-faith report or participating in an investigation is prohibited.

---

## 11. Information Security

Employees are responsible for protecting company systems, accounts, devices, and data.

Employees must:

- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable multi-factor authentication when required.
- Never share passwords or authentication codes.
- Lock their workstation when unattended.
- Install software only through approved processes.
- Keep operating systems and applications updated.
- Report suspected phishing or security incidents.
- Use company data only for authorized purposes.
- Avoid storing confidential information in unapproved services.
- Follow access-control and least-privilege requirements.

Employees must not:

- Bypass security controls without authorization.
- Access data unrelated to their responsibilities.
- Copy company data to personal devices without approval.
- Use another person's account.
- Disable security software without authorization.

---

## 12. Data Privacy and Confidentiality

Employees may have access to personal data, customer information, employee information, business plans, source code, financial information, and other confidential material.

Confidential information must be:

- Accessed only for legitimate work purposes.
- Shared only with authorized recipients.
- Stored using approved systems.
- Protected against accidental disclosure.
- Deleted or returned according to retention requirements.

Personal data should be collected and used only for a defined and legitimate purpose. Employees should avoid collecting unnecessary information.

If confidential information is sent to the wrong person, lost, exposed, or accessed improperly, the employee must report the incident immediately through the approved security or privacy reporting channel.

Confidentiality obligations may continue after employment ends.

---

## 13. Acceptable Use of Technology

Company technology is provided primarily for business purposes.

Reasonable personal use may be permitted if it:

- Does not interfere with work.
- Does not create security or legal risk.
- Does not consume excessive resources.
- Does not violate company policy.
- Does not involve inappropriate or illegal content.

Employees must not use company systems to:

- Harass or threaten others.
- Distribute malicious software.
- Access unlawful content.
- Conduct unauthorized security testing.
- Infringe intellectual-property rights.
- Run unauthorized commercial activities.
- Circumvent monitoring or access controls.

The organization may monitor or review the use of company systems where permitted by applicable law and company policy.

---

## 14. Artificial Intelligence Tools

Employees may use artificial intelligence tools only in accordance with approved company guidance.

Before entering information into an external AI service, employees must determine whether the information contains:

- Personal data.
- Customer data.
- Confidential business information.
- Source code subject to restrictions.
- Credentials or secrets.
- Regulated or sensitive information.

Employees must not enter passwords, API keys, access tokens, private customer information, or confidential company documents into an unapproved AI tool.

AI-generated output must be reviewed by a qualified employee before it is used for:

- Customer communication.
- Production code.
- Legal or compliance decisions.
- Financial decisions.
- Security-sensitive work.
- Employment decisions.
- Public statements.

Employees remain responsible for the accuracy, legality, security, and appropriateness of work produced with AI assistance.

---

## 15. Intellectual Property

Work created by employees in the course of their employment may be subject to intellectual-property provisions in the employment agreement and applicable law.

Employees must:

- Follow company rules for source code and documentation.
- Use approved repositories.
- Respect third-party licenses.
- Avoid copying proprietary material without authorization.
- Document the origin of external code or assets.
- Obtain approval before publishing company-owned material.

Open-source software must be used in accordance with its license and company policy.

---

## 16. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest may occur when personal interests interfere, or appear to interfere, with an employee's ability to act in the organization's best interests.

Potential conflicts may involve:

- Outside employment.
- Financial interests in suppliers or competitors.
- Personal relationships in reporting lines.
- Gifts or benefits from vendors.
- Using company resources for personal business.
- Hiring or supervising close relatives without appropriate safeguards.

Employees must disclose potential conflicts promptly to their manager or Human Resources. A disclosed conflict can often be managed through reassignment, transparency, approval, or other controls.

---

## 17. Gifts, Hospitality, and Anti-Bribery

Employees must not offer, request, or accept bribes, kickbacks, or improper benefits.

Gifts and hospitality must be:

- Reasonable.
- Infrequent.
- Transparent.
- Consistent with applicable law.
- Unrelated to an expectation of improper influence.
- Reported or approved when required by policy.

Cash gifts and secret commissions are prohibited.

When uncertain, employees should decline the benefit and consult the appropriate compliance contact.

---

## 18. Workplace Health and Safety

Employees must follow workplace health and safety requirements.

Employees should:

- Report hazards and accidents promptly.
- Keep work areas clean and safe.
- Use equipment correctly.
- Follow emergency procedures.
- Participate in required safety training.
- Report unsafe behavior.
- Avoid creating risks for colleagues, customers, or visitors.

In an emergency, employees should follow instructions from emergency personnel and the organization's safety procedures.

---

## 19. Business Travel and Expenses

Business expenses must be necessary, reasonable, properly documented, and connected to approved business activity.

Employees submitting expenses should provide:

- Date.
- Business purpose.
- Amount.
- Currency.
- Receipt where required.
- Names of participants when relevant.
- Manager or budget-owner approval when required.

Personal expenses must not be submitted as business expenses.

Travel bookings should follow the approved travel process. Employees should consider cost, safety, business necessity, and applicable travel rules.

---

## 20. Communication and Collaboration

Employees should use approved communication channels for work-related information.

Good communication includes:

- Clear and respectful messages.
- Appropriate urgency.
- Accurate information.
- Useful documentation.
- Timely escalation of blockers.
- Awareness of time zones.
- Respect for focused work and reasonable boundaries.

Important decisions should be documented in an accessible company system rather than remaining only in private messages.

Employees should avoid hostile, insulting, discriminatory, or threatening communication.

---

## 21. Reporting Concerns

Employees are encouraged to report suspected misconduct, security incidents, privacy incidents, safety hazards, discrimination, harassment, fraud, or policy violations.

A report should include, where possible:

- What happened.
- When it happened.
- Where it happened.
- Who was involved.
- Available evidence.
- Potential impact.
- Immediate actions already taken.

Employees should report urgent safety or security risks immediately through the appropriate emergency or incident channel.

Good-faith reports are protected from retaliation. Employees should not knowingly submit false reports.

---

## 22. Corrective Action

When an employee violates a policy or fails to meet reasonable expectations, the organization may use corrective action.

Depending on the seriousness and circumstances, corrective action may include:

1. Informal coaching.
2. Additional training.
3. A documented warning.
4. A performance-improvement plan.
5. Suspension where permitted.
6. Termination of employment where justified.

The organization should consider the facts, severity, prior conduct, applicable law, and relevant employment terms.

Serious misconduct may result in immediate escalation.

---

## 23. Separation from the Organization

When employment ends, employees must:

- Return company equipment and property.
- Return or securely delete confidential information as instructed.
- Transfer work, documentation, and responsibilities.
- Revoke or surrender access credentials as required.
- Submit outstanding expense claims.
- Complete exit procedures.
- Respect continuing confidentiality and intellectual-property obligations.

Managers should ensure that access rights are removed promptly and that important work is transferred appropriately.

## 24. Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I request vacation?

Submit a request through the approved leave-management process and wait for the required approval.

### What should I do if I will be late?

Notify your manager or team contact as soon as possible and provide an estimated arrival time.

### Where do I report a security incident?

Use the approved security incident channel immediately. If you are unsure, contact IT or the security team.

### Can I use an AI tool with company documents?

Only if the tool is approved for that type of information and the use complies with company security, privacy, and AI policies.

### What should I do if I experience harassment?

Document the concern if safe to do so and report it to your manager, Human Resources, or another designated reporting channel.

### What if I make a mistake?

Report it promptly, explain the impact, take reasonable steps to contain it, and cooperate in correcting the problem.

### Who can answer policy questions?

Your manager, Human Resources, Legal, Compliance, IT, Security, or another designated policy owner can provide guidance depending on the subject.

## 25. Document Ownership and Review

Human Resources owns the general employee handbook. Individual policies may be owned by departments such as Legal, Security, Finance, IT, or Compliance.

The handbook should be reviewed periodically and updated when:

- Laws or regulations change.
- Company processes change.
- New risks are identified.
- A policy becomes unclear or outdated.
- An incident reveals a control weakness.

Employees are responsible for reading updated policies and completing required acknowledgments or training.
